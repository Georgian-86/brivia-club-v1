## Ruling A1: honest caps versus D-019 silent caps

**Ruling: compromise. Be honest about the sender's own quota, and stay silent about every recipient-side reason.**

1. **The sender's quota is honest.** A member sees "N signals left today" and, at zero, "More at HH:MM". These come
   from a new `my_signal_quota()` RPC. Over the cap, the send fails *visibly*: the card is not consumed, and the
   member is told their quota is used. The 100-live-unanswered cap gets the same treatment ("You have 100 signals
   waiting for an answer").
2. **The quota counts the sender's attempts, never outcomes.** One `send_signal(p_to, p_note)` SECURITY DEFINER RPC
   replaces the raw insert. It records every attempt in a sender-only ledger before looking at the recipient. These
   cases all cost one unit and all return the same "Signal sent":
   - blocked in either direction;
   - the other test world;
   - a duplicate or live earlier request (23505);
   - a recipient at capacity;
   - an earlier decline.

   The counter can't move differently for any recipient-side state, so it can't be used as a probe. A request that
   completes a match is never refused (D-019 is kept), and it still costs one unit.
3. **Recipient-side reasons stay silent.** Nothing tells a sender that a recipient blocked them, declined, is saturated,
   or is in another world. This keeps D-017 and D-019's decline indistinguishability, and it fixes J3.
4. **One server counter (A2).** The localStorage 15-swipe limit is removed. Passes are free. The deck size (8–12)
   limits browsing, and the server quota limits signals. The default stays at 30 per 24 h as a private config
   value. Worth-the-Distance and long-range signals get their own server counters (spec §7: 5 per week).

**Rationale.**
- A false "Signal sent" at the cap is a lie to the member. Members who reach their cap are the most engaged, so it
  hits them hardest.
- The abuse value of hiding the cap is near zero. A spammer finds it in one day by checking `my_outgoing_requests`,
  and the client already shows a limit (J2).
- What really needs hiding is recipient state. Charging every attempt the same hides it fully.
- The spammer still gets no extra throughput, because the cap holds either way.

**Dissent preserved.**
- **D-019 / Ruling I8 (the position A1 overturns):** a cap that is never revealed gives a scripted sender nothing
  to pace against, and keeps the number itself private. The judge's steelman of that position is to show the counter
  only near the cap (say, from 5 remaining) with a coarse reset time. The judge **adopts the coarse reset time** (to
  the hour) and **rejects the threshold**. A member who doesn't know their budget can't spend it well. Pacing at a
  known cap is no worse than pacing at a discovered one, and this cap is discoverable in a day anyway.
- **A** asked for a Sent tab alongside the counter. It is P1 below and not a blocker.

This **supersedes D-019 in part**: the silent drop at the cap and the "same Signal sent" behaviour for the sender's
own quota. The caps, expiry, decline masking and block withdrawal in D-019 stand.

## Prioritised backlog for iteration 3 (judge)

Scope rule: one iteration delivers the core promise *in the data and UI*, plus a location-first deck. The ORBIT
service (R, the gate, the Roche Limit) goes live in iteration 4 behind the §9.1 trust boundary, after the C1 study.
ORBIT formulas are **not** ported into SQL. Two implementations would drift, and the spec allows one.

**P0, required for go-live**

   M3, M4, M5, and the cached plaintext password.

2. **Onboarding, the core promise (A3).** A taxonomy seed (spec phase 1: about 400 nodes), a Passion Budget of 20
   points with modes, and "Your orbit" location.
   - Location uses geolocation, with a privacy explainer *before* the browser prompt, or "Pick my city" (centroid).
     Either way it goes through `set_home_location`, which snaps server-side to an H3 res-7 cell.

   - Free-text City/State is removed from sign-up.

     - A new member can't complete a profile without at least 1 interest, all 20 points spent and a cell.
     - No column, log or response holds coordinates (grep and DB check).

4. **A location-first interim deck.** Replace `list_members` in the deck with `deck_candidates(p_limit ≤ 20)`, a

     tests at N = 0, 5 and 2000.
   - **C2 + J1:** the liker bar is 14 days, completed and unflagged, matching §9.1.4. Load from not-yet-proven likers

   - a golden test where a 4-interest pair with 2 shared interests at ring 3 is eligible at full liquidity;

## Go-live gate for real members

Real sign-ups may reach the live Supabase project only when **all** of the following hold. A closed beta in one
launch city is allowed once the gate passes. Open sign-ups also wait for the ORBIT service (iteration 4).

1. **Security fixes landed.** Every iteration-2 final-review finding (I1, I2, M3, M4, M5, cached password) is fixed,
   with a harness test each.
2. **Migrations.** All migrations are applied in order to the live project. `bash supabase/tests/run.sh` is green on
   the same SQL. The Supabase security advisor shows no table without RLS and no SECURITY DEFINER function
   executable by `anon` beyond what the plan allows.
3. **Client secrets.** The client bundle holds only the anon key. A grep of `dist/` finds no `service_role`, no
   password, and no email or phone of another member in any response (contract test).
4. **Location privacy.** No coordinates are stored or logged anywhere. `set_home_location` is POST-only, rate-limited
   and excluded from body capture. Cards show distance bands only. The k-anonymity rule is live.
5. **Consent.** A match exists only through mutual consent (the 0002 harness tests are green). Signal quotas are
   enforced server-side, and recipient-side outcomes can't be told apart (the P0-3 test).
6. **Test world.** Seeded members are `is_test`. A real-member session sees zero test rows on every RPC (harness).
   The one-transaction purge is rehearsed on a copy.
7. **Auth hardening.** Email confirmation is on, with a minimum password length, CAPTCHA on sign-up and auth rate
   limits. No password is cached client-side.
8. **Member safety.** Block, report and account deletion work end to end. Deletion removes the profile, storage
   objects, requests and matches. A moderation contact is published.
9. **Legal.** A privacy notice and terms are published. The notice covers coarse location, the interaction log and
   impression logging, and consent wording fits the launch jurisdiction (for India, the DPDP Act 2023). The
   geolocation explainer comes before the browser prompt.
10. **Operations.** Backups or PITR are on, error tracking scrubs PII, an incident owner is named, and the repos
    are private (IP_NOTES).
11. **Sign-off.** The founder signs off, recorded as a DECISIONS entry that names the commit and the migration set
    applied.
