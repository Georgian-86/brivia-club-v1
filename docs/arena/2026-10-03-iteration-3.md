### R1: interim deck ordering and pool membership (the main conflict)

**Invariant (new, normative):** a target's pool membership and its position may depend only on what the card shows
(its band, its shared-interest chips) and on the viewer's own state. They may never depend on a finer location
than the band shows.

**Pool membership.**
1. A **fine target** is one where both members have `precision = 'cell'` and the target's g7 cell is `ok10`. It is
   admitted when the g7-to-g7 distance is 15 km or less (rings 0–1). Its band is `~3 km` or `~10 km`. Those edges are
   exactly what the label already discloses, at a level where the cell holds at least 10 counted members.
2. **Every other admission** uses the place rule. The target's place centroid must be within 60 km of the viewer's
   place centroid. The band is the target's place name, or region, or "Abroad". Moving inside one place can never
   change membership, so walking the pool reveals at most the target's place, which the card already shows.
3. No member is ever admitted on the true ring 2 at g7 again. The 60 km edge at g7 precision is the B-F1 oracle.

**Order.**
1. `shared_any desc`: at least one shared, active, **non-sensitive** exact interest.
2. Display ring asc: 0 (`~3 km`), then 1 (`~10 km`), then the place tier.
3. Budget-bounded overlap `Σ min(p_viewer, p_target) / 20` desc, over the same non-sensitive exact ids. It is
   breadth-neutral and cannot be gamed by adding interests (C-3).
4. A daily rotating tie key, `md5(caller || target || current_date)`. Propensity is known and the head of the deck
   rotates (C-4).

Members with no shared interest still appear, after every member who shares one.

**Why this resolves the conflict.** The north star says the default feed shows "people with similar interests in the
same area" (CLAUDE.md, rules 1–2). Interest qualifies a person; location orders the qualified people. A ring-0
stranger with nothing in common does not meet "similar interests", so leading with them is not location-first in the
product's own terms. C's evidence also shows that the exact-count key rewards spreading points thinly, which taints
C1 data. The privacy closure does not depend on this choice: keys 1 and 2 are both functions of what the card shows.

**Rejected:**
- C's proposal kept the true-ring pool. That alone leaves B-F1 open.
- B's 24 h settling delay after a move is unnecessary once membership is coarse, and it hurts real movers. It is P2
  defence in depth only.
- "True ring within a band that already shows km" (D-034 open item) is subsumed by the fine-target rule.

**Supporting P0 changes:**
- A `like` or `pass` interaction row is accepted only for a target served to the viewer in the last 7 days (deck,
  search or `get_candidates`). Otherwise it is silently ignored. This also gives C-4 a clean denominator.
- The probe becomes a harness test (`orbit-triangulation.test.sql`). It uses sybils, pass-isolation and an edge walk,
  and asserts that, for a non-fine target, membership is constant across every viewer point inside one place.

### R3: the sensitive interest set

**Make these public** (C): `wellbeing.health.nutrition`, `.sleep` and `.healthy_ageing`. They are lifestyle interests,
not health data. Moving them restores chips and overlap for ordinary wellness matches.

**Make these sensitive** (B):
- `music.listening.sufi_qawwali` (a religion proxy; its sibling is already sensitive);

**Completion floor (B-F4):** completion requires at least one non-sensitive interest. Check it in
`set_member_interests` and in `brivia_member_completed`. B's 50 % share rule is rejected. It over-constrains, and
point shares are never exposed.

**Separate consent (DPDP):**
- Picking any sensitive interest requires a separate, explicit, withdrawable consent (`profiles.sensitive_consent_at`).
- One action withdraws the consent and deletes all sensitive rows.
- Onboarding says sensitive points "are private and don't affect who you see yet" (C-7).

**ORBIT firewall (C-2):**
- Topology nodes carry `sensitive`.
- The displayed R, the gate, the order and `explain` use non-sensitive hits only, until a future mutual opt-in.
- A contract test asserts that changing only a sensitive interest leaves every card's `matchPercent`, chips and
  position unchanged.
- `ORBIT_ENGINE.md:737-740` and D-029 are amended in the same commit.
- The interest-rewrite cap is 3 accepted rewrites per rolling 24 h after first completion.

**Dissent preserved:**

  critics flagged it and the cost is a few chips.
- **B** would have made public policy and climate action consent-gated now. The judge files that as P2 (the toggle).

## Recommendation: amend 0004 or add 0005

**Amend 0004. The founder should not run 0004 against the live project yet.**

Reasons:
- Nothing from `0001`–`0004` is live, so a single canonical `0004` is cheaper and safer than an `0004` with a
  corrective `0005`.
- Test members on the live project would show nothing that the local harness does not.
- Running 0004 now would freeze it. Every R1–R4 change would then need to be written as an `alter` in `0005`, and the
  harness and the runbook would carry both.

The P0-A list below is one iteration task (SQL, tests and spec), plus the client copy.

**Rule (record in D-038):** a migration file that has been applied to the live project is frozen. All later changes
go in a new numbered file.

**Fallback.** If the founder has already run 0004, or chooses to run it now with test members only, that is safe. No
real person is in the world, so B-F1 and A-F2 have no victims. In that case 0004 is frozen, and P0-A ships as
`0005_iteration4_privacy.sql` with the same acceptance criteria. Real members still wait for the gate.
